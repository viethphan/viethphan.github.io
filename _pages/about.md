Greetings! I am Viet from Viet Nam. I am currently a PhD candidate in Sociology at Boston College. I use a mix of quantitative and qualitative methods to study 
the different aspects of the ongoing global efforts towards reducing carbon emissions. My work has appeared in *Social Forces* and *Environmental Sociology*.

My dissertation focuses on the environmental implications of financialization, defined broadly as the increasing importance of financial institutions, markets, and motivations in the economy. The three papers in my dissertation tackle how financialization can unintentionally lead to a fall in emssions level, how financial firms respond to new regulations that ask them to estimate and disclose climate-related impacts, and how the increase in the use of corporate debt can hamper environmental policies design to curb firm-level emissions. Together, these papers paint an intricate picture, showing that not only does financialization matter for decarbonization in multiple ways and across different levels of analysis, its impacts are also complex and often contradictory.

My other work centers more broadly on the political economy of decarbonization. My recent project examines the varying impacts of renewable energy consumption across different levels of income inequality and GDP per capita. I have also collaborated with colleagues from Boston College on a cross-national comparative study theorizing the common levers that make climate transition possible across different states. Other ongoing collaborative projects include a paper on how early adopters respond to "nature-related financial disclosure" frameworks (with Janna Huang), a project on the relationship between financialization at the household level and consumption-based emissions (with Xiaorui Huang), and an anlaysis of climate discourses in earnings calls from financial firms (with Annika Rieger).
 
My research has been supported by the Morrissey Graduate School Dissertation Research Fellowship (AY 26-27).

Prior to graduate school, I worked in the consulting industry in Singapore for four years after receiving an MA at York University (Canada) and a BA at Singapore Management University. Outside of research, I like to practice classical guitar and enjoy drawing.
