***Abstract***: Existing works on the renewable energy-inequality-national income nexus suggest two potential kinds of trade-offs. The first concerns national income level, which shows that renewable energy’s decarbonization effect weakens among richer countries. The second, still under debate, concerns inequality reduction, which can similarly weaken renewable energy effectiveness according to the Marginal Propensity to Emit (MPE) perspective. I consider these trade-offs jointly and examine the “green cost”—defined as the decreasing effectiveness of renewable energy for decarbonization—of inequality reduction across different national income levels. Using a panel dataset of 175 countries from 1990 to 2021, I estimate two-way fixed-effects models with three-way interaction terms between income concentration at the top, renewable energy consumption, and GDP per capita to examine the marginal effects of renewable energy on emissions across different combinations of inequality and GDP per capita. There are three main results. First, the decarbonizing effects of renewable energy become weaker as GPD per capita increases, suggesting a persisting green cost. Second, I find that in poorer countries, inequality accentuates the decarbonizing effects of renewable energy, suggesting a trade-off between decarbonization and more equitable income distribution, i.e., a green cost to inequality reduction. Thirdly, and in contrast, for higher-income countries, higher inequality can weaken the effects of renewable energy consumption, suggesting that the green cost of inequality reduction becomes increasingly irrelevant as a country grows richer. These results highlight potential complications around decarbonization and growth strategies centering on renewable energy deployment, the necessity of considering post-growth strategies, and the importance of addressing inequality in ongoing low-carbon transitions.
