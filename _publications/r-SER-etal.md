This paper is under review at *Socio-Economic Review*.'

***Abstract***: What are the conditions under which states pursue decarbonization transitions? Building on existing research which highlights interest politics, economic structure, and state capacity, we present four case studies analyzing defining moments of national climate transitions in China, France, Singapore, and the United States. Our cases suggest that while national trajectories take different forms, they share a set of four enabling conditions. Two have been commonly referenced in past research: a country’s positions vis-a-vis fossil fuels and low-carbon energy sources. These are key to achieving alignment between growth and decarbonization, thus enabling a climate transition. Two other conditions are less frequently analyzed. The first is geopolitical ambition, to be distinguished from global geoeconomic competition. The second is that climate transitions involve the adoption of a novel discursive model of a successful low-carbon economy. We find that a state’s understanding of its viable pathway plays a prominent role in the ongoing transition.
